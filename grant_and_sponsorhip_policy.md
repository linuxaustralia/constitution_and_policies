# Linux Australia Grant and Sponsorship Policy


## Introduction

As a Not-for-Profit organisation, Linux Australia's excess operating funds
are used to benefit the Free and Open Source (FOSS) community (and thus the
general public) in line with the Linux Australia (LA) Constitution and
Objects.  Grants and Sponsorships are one way this is done.  This policy
defines the process associated with grants and sponsorships.

## Funding

The funds allocated for grants and sponsorships shall be determined by the
LA Council.  The most significant factor influencing the amount made
available is the profit realised in the previous LA financial year.  If LA
posted a loss over the previous year the LA Council may decide to suspend
the grant and sponsorship program for 12 months.


## Timing

In years where there is funding for grants and sponsorships, the LA Council
will open the grants program and publicise the available funds as early as
practical following Council elections.  The grants program will remain open
until mid-November or until the funds are exhausted (whichever occurs
first).


## Types of Grants and Sponsorships

Linux Australia offers grants in two categories.

  * _Project Grants_: funding open source, open data, open government, open
    education, open hardware and open culture projects for the following
    purposes.

      - Overhead costs such as hardware or bandwidth
      - Resources such as web space, hosting and mailing lists
      - Advertising or media coverage of an event or conference
      - Bulk ordering of items that may be useful to user groups or special
        interest groups
      - Support costs for a community project that uses open technologies
      - Other costs related to the delivery of the project or grant
        objective, within reason

  * _Community Grants_: funds to assist groups within our community with their
    activities.  Funds can be used to help cover expenses associated with
    open technology community activities, such as:

      - Infrastructure to support in-person or virtual meetings
      - Room hire or staging costs for gatherings
      - Catering for in-person events
      - Publicity and promotion of community activities or events
      - Reasonable costs may be included in the grant application for labour
        or professional services.

Sponsorships are normally handled as Project Grants.


## Grant Application Process

The grant application process generally follows the steps below.

 1. Grant applications should be submitted using the application form on
    the LA website.  This form will be available to members of Linux
    Australia while the grants program is open.

 2. The complete grant submission will be made available to LA members for a
    period of two weeks to obtain community feedback.  Community members may
    ask questions about the grant which applicants are encouraged to answer.

 3. The LA Council will consider the grant application at its first
    scheduled meeting after the close of the consultation period.  Council
    may choose to:

      - Award the grant
      - Decline the application
      - Seek more information from the applicant

 4. The LA Council will communicate the outcome of the grant application
    within 7 days of the meeting at which the decision is made.

 5. The applicant will work with the LA Treasurer to access the funds.

At its discretion the LA Council may vary the application process under
compelling circumstances.


## Application Guidelines and Criteria

LA Council encourages applicants to get in touch before submitting their
grant if they would like guidance or feedback on their proposal.

Feedback received during the community consultation shall be taken into
account by the LA Council when evaluating a grant application, alongside the
following criteria.

  * The grant application is consistent with LA's values and constitution.

  * All resources produced with the help of Linux Australia grants should be
    made available to the public under an open technology or documentation
    licence acceptable to the Linux Australia Council.

  * Background information about the group or individual behind the grant
    application is useful, particularly if it is not being done by a
    well-established Open Source project or group.  This makes it easier for
    the LA Council to determine how the project fits into the broader
    community.

  * A LA grant should target a specific activity or event.  While
    applications for general support will be considered, such a proposal
    would need to include a detailed explanation of how the funds would
    ultimately benefit the broader Open Source community (rather than a
    select few associated with the grant).

  * A grant application should include an explanation as to how the
    requested amount was determined.  While formal quotations are not
    generally required, realistic estimates should be used wherever
    possible.

  * Within the total funding pool available for grants and sponsorships,
    there are no specific limits on the value of the grants.  However,
    grants which request larger sums of money (for example, amounts close to
    or above AUD 5000) should include more detail and can expect to attract
    greater scrutiny.

  * The grant should be clearly linked to and benefit open technologies or
    the FOSS community.

For grants that include the funding of hardware purchases to support Open
Source software development:

  * The grant application should include a description of what the project
    aims to achieve with the funded hardware.  For example:

      – Support for this hardware is committed to the upstream project
        repository.

      – A sustainable community of developers is formed to continue
        development and support of the hardware.

      – An individual takes an active role in the development of Open
        Source support for the hardware, which would have otherwise been
        impossible due to the cost of the hardware.

  * An individual or group may receive at most one grant of this nature per
    grant cycle.

Grant applications which include the following are unlikely to be accepted.

  * Contributions to the general revenue stream of an organisation or
    company.

  * Coverage of expenses associated with the operational overheads of a
    company.

  * Funding of activities which provide no obvious benefits to the broader
    Open Source community.

  * Development of hardware or software which will not be released under an
    open licence.

  * Applications which lack justification and explanation of the amount
    requested.


## Report for Linux Australia Annual Report

The applicant of a successful grant application shall provide a written
report to LA Council for inclusion in Linux Australia's Annual report by
early to mid November.  Extensions may be granted for grants awarded late in
the year or for grants whose activities are not completed by November.  This
report should be written to inform LA members about the activity funded by
the grant, how the grant assisted the applicant, and anything else the
community may be interested to know about.  Photos cleared for publication
are welcomed.

The report should include a review of what went well and what did not should
be included.  If the project’s objectives were not met, it should explain
what might have helped the project achieve more.  If applicable, follow up
activities should be described which could build on the progress made and
offer guidance on how others might be able to assist to provide additional
value for the broader community.  For grants that include the funding of
hardware purchases to support Open Source software development, the future
plans for the funded hardware should also be provided.
